Research

We study why tissues age unevenly.

Damage that is never fully repaired leaves behind small aging niches, making aging a non-linear, mosaic process. We follow these niches, the cells that change fate inside them, and the diseases they lead to.

1 Aging environments

Which tissue environments make cells age? In which organs — and in what order — do aging microenvironments emerge?

What we do

We build single-cell multi-omic atlases to determine the order in which organs age and the changes that arise along the way.

Key papers

  • Quasi-spatial single-cell transcriptome based on physical tissue properties defines early aging associated niche in liver
    Tak et al., Nature Aging, 2025
  • Transcriptomic Analysis of Cellular Senescence: One Step Closer to Senescence Atlas
    Kim et al., Molecules and Cells, 2021

2 Cell-fate decisions

How does a cell integrate gene regulation, signaling and metabolism to choose senescence over other fates?

What we do

We follow cells longitudinally as they age, in cell and animal models, to define how the senescence fate decision is made, and we run genetic screens for factors that can reverse it.

Key papers

  • Distinct characteristics of two types of alternative lengthening of telomeres in mouse embryonic stem cells
    Sung et al., Nucleic Acids Research, 2023
  • Telomeres reforged with non-telomeric sequences in mouse embryonic stem cells
    Kim et al., Nature Communications, 2021

3 From fate to disease

How do these fate decisions feed back to build fibrotic, inflamed and declining tissues?

What we do

We model imperfect repair to understand, at spatial single-cell resolution, how aging microenvironments form.

Key papers

  • Imperfect repair in aging: senescent cells and the hepatic fibrotic niche
    Kim et al., BMB Reports, 2026
  • Neutrophil-derived itaconate facilitates tiered pulmonary inflammation via Kdm5b-associated epigenetic remodeling in alveolar macrophages
    Lim et al., Cell Reports, 2026
